# Technology Value Scorecard & Executive Reporting

A good executive scorecard combines financial control with enterprise outcomes.

## Recommended scorecard

| Lens | Example measures |
|---|---|
| Financial | budget variance, forecast accuracy, savings realised, TCO |
| Productivity | hours/capacity returned, cycle time, automation, rework |
| Risk & Resilience | control coverage, recovery results, critical risks |
| Experience | service quality, adoption, satisfaction, friction |
| Strategic Capability | capabilities enabled, roadmap progress, constraints removed |

## Executive narrative

Avoid reporting a wall of technology activity.

A useful monthly or quarterly narrative is:

1. **Money** — where are we against budget and forecast?
2. **Value** — what measurable benefit has been realised?
3. **Risk** — what exposure changed?
4. **Capability** — what did we enable for the organisation?
5. **Decision** — what requires executive choice?

## Example

Weak:
> Migrated 80% of users and closed 47 tickets.

Stronger:
> Consolidation is tracking to $180K annualised saving. Migration is 80% complete, three legacy contracts are scheduled for retirement, and support accountability has moved to two strategic providers. The main remaining value risk is delayed decommissioning of two duplicated services.

Activity becomes evidence in support of an outcome, not the outcome itself.
