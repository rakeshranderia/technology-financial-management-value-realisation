# Technology Business Cases & ROI

A technology business case should describe the problem, baseline, options, total cost, expected value, risks and ownership.

## Basic structure

1. Business problem / opportunity
2. Current-state baseline
3. Do-nothing consequence
4. Options considered
5. Total cost of ownership
6. Expected benefits
7. Risks and dependencies
8. Adoption/change requirements
9. Measures and benefit owners
10. Review dates

## ROI where it is meaningful

Simple ROI:

**(Benefit - Cost) / Cost**

Other useful financial measures:
- payback period;
- NPV;
- annualised saving;
- cost avoidance;
- cost per transaction/user/service.

## Where conventional ROI is weak

Security, resilience, compliance and foundational capability often have uncertain or indirect returns.

Do not solve this by inventing precision.

Instead state:
- exposure being addressed;
- consequence of inaction;
- control or resilience improvement;
- business capability enabled;
- cost and alternatives;
- residual risk.

## Value hypothesis

Every investment should be able to state:

> If we invest **X**, we expect **Y outcome** because **Z changes**, measured by **A/B/C**, owned by **named business and technology stakeholders**.

That is more useful than a spreadsheet with unsupported assumptions.
