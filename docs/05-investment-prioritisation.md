# Investment Prioritisation

Technology demand normally exceeds available money and delivery capacity.

Prioritisation should therefore compare initiatives on a common basis.

## Suggested dimensions

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Business outcome | What measurable outcome is affected? |
| Financial value | What saving, avoided cost or revenue impact exists? |
| Productivity | What capacity or throughput changes? |
| Risk | What exposure is reduced or created? |
| Strategic alignment | Which enterprise priority does this support? |
| Urgency | Is there a deadline, lifecycle event or dependency? |
| Dependency | What other initiatives depend on this? |
| Readiness | Are process, data, ownership and adoption ready? |
| Cost | What is full lifecycle cost? |
| Delivery capacity | Can the organisation absorb the change? |

## Mandatory versus discretionary

Not every initiative should be scored as though optional.

Separate:
- mandatory regulatory or contractual work;
- critical lifecycle/resilience work;
- discretionary growth/optimisation;
- experiments and innovation.

This prevents a superficially high ROI initiative from displacing an essential control or lifecycle requirement.

## Portfolio balance

A healthy portfolio balances:
- run sustainability;
- risk reduction;
- efficiency;
- growth;
- transformation;
- experimentation.

Prioritisation is not choosing the mathematically highest score. It is creating a transparent decision record.
