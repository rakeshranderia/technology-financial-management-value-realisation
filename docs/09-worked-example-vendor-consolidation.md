# Worked Example: Vendor Consolidation

This example is illustrative but grounded in a common enterprise technology pattern.

## Starting point

A technology function uses 18 separate vendors across support, infrastructure, security and specialist services.

Problems:
- duplicated services;
- fragmented accountability;
- inconsistent escalation;
- management overhead;
- overlapping contracts;
- difficult service measurement.

## Baseline

Example measures:
- annual vendor cost;
- number of vendors/contracts;
- internal hours spent on vendor coordination;
- incident hand-offs;
- unresolved ownership/escalation issues;
- duplicated platforms/services.

## Intervention

Consolidate the estate into two strategic partners with clear service boundaries, scorecards and commercial accountability.

## Tangible value

- approximately $180K annualised saving;
- fewer contract renewals;
- reduced duplicate service cost.

## Intangible / operational value

- clearer accountability;
- fewer hand-offs;
- improved escalation;
- stronger service visibility;
- simpler governance;
- reduced internal coordination overhead;
- improved resilience through clearer ownership.

## Better value narrative

Not just:

> IT saved $180K.

But:

> Vendor consolidation reduced annual technology cost by approximately $180K while simplifying the operating model, reducing hand-offs and establishing clearer service accountability and escalation.

That is the difference between cost reporting and value reporting.
